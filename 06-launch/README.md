### Slide 6 · Research
- **Competitive analysis:** Responsible Payers currently use a combination of treatment plans, invoices, financing offers, and direct communication with practices to understand their financial commitment. Unlike modern financing and payment experiences that provide a single source of truth, the current experience requires customers to manually piece together information across multiple documents and touchpoints.

As a result, customers often create their own workarounds, including personal calculations, spreadsheets, and direct support inquiries to determine what is financed, who is responsible for payment, and how much remains outstanding. This fragmented experience contributes to the current post-creation manual contact rate of 14.6%.
- **Journey map:** 1. Customer receives treatment plan or invoice and considers financing options.

2. Customer reviews the installment agreement and decides whether to commit.

3. Customer tries to understand what is financed, who is responsible for payment, and what the long-term financial obligation will be.

4. Moment of Misery:
The customer cannot clearly determine what the installment plan covers, how payments may change over time, or what balance remains outstanding.

5. Customer manually combines information from invoices, treatment plans, financing documents, and conversations with the provider.

6. Customer contacts support or the provider for clarification.

7. Customer monitors the agreement after creation and may require additional clarification when plan adjustments, extensions, or payment changes occur.

## Audit, override & decide
- **Where did you override the AI? (feature + old vs. new score + why):** I overrode three AI scores where our M2/M3 evidence was stronger than the generic assessment. I increased B2, Financing Scope Breakdown, from Value 4 to 5 because M2 interviews explicitly showed that payers do not understand whether the plan covers total treatment cost, an invoice, or a co-payment. I increased B3, Installment Plan Change Timeline, from Value 4 to 5 because 14.6% of agreements require manual contact after creation and plan extensions and reductions are recurring post-creation events. I also increased B7, Resolve a Returned Direct Debit, from Value 4 to 5 because contacted agreements have a 34.35% direct-debit return rate compared with 13.00% overall. These overrides shift the roadmap toward payment transparency and lifecycle understanding rather than a purely transactional self-service backlog.
- **Did the AI over-value a Sales/Eng request your M2 interviews don’t support?:** Yes. The AI initially over-valued self-service transaction features such as Early Settlement and Extra Payment because they are visible customer requests and relatively easy to understand. However, our M2 interviews did not show that customers primarily struggled to make additional payments or settle early. Instead, customers consistently struggled to understand what was financed, who was responsible for payment, and how their obligation changed over time.

As a result, I increased the priority of transparency-focused features such as Payment Commitment Overview, Financing Scope Breakdown, Installment Plan Change Timeline, and Plan Change Explanations, while reducing the relative priority of convenience-oriented features. Our M3 data further supported this shift, showing that 14.6% of agreements require post-creation manual contact, indicating that understanding and managing the agreement lifecycle is a bigger problem than adding transactional flexibility.
- **Did it underweight something your M3 cohort/funnel data strongly supports?:** Yes. The AI initially underweighted lifecycle transparency features such as Installment Plan Change Timeline, Plan Change Status Tracking, and “Why Did My Plan Change?” explanations. While these features may appear less transactional than self-service actions, our M3 data showed that 14.6% of installment agreements require manual contact after creation and that more than 12,000 agreements were either extended or shortened. This suggests that customers struggle less with executing actions and more with understanding changes after the agreement has been created.

As a result, I increased the value of features that create transparency around plan changes, status, and payment obligations over time. The data indicates that reducing post-creation confusion is likely to have a greater impact on manual-contact reduction than adding additional transactional self-service capabilities.

### Slide 10 · Story
- **Friction + aha:** Friction Points

My initial assumption was that customers primarily needed more self-service functionality, such as changing installments, updating payment details, or managing agreement changes themselves. However, both the interviews and the data showed a different picture. Customers were not mainly struggling to perform actions; they were struggling to understand their financial commitment in the first place.

The biggest challenge throughout the project was consistently separating symptoms from root causes. Manual contacts, payment adjustments, and agreement changes initially appeared to be operational problems. After combining qualitative interviews with quantitative data, it became clear that many of these issues were actually caused by a lack of transparency and understanding.

Aha Moment

The biggest insight was realizing that understanding comes before self-service. Customers were not asking for more controls; they were asking for more clarity. The moment I connected the 14.6% post-creation manual contact rate with interview feedback about confusion around financed amounts, payment responsibility, and changing obligations, the solution became much simpler.

Instead of building a broader self-service portal, I focused on creating a single source of truth for the Responsible Payer. This shift transformed the initiative from a feature-heavy servicing project into a transparency-focused customer experience improvement.
- **Takeaways / next:** Key Takeaways

- Customer confusion, not missing functionality, was the primary driver of post-creation support contacts.
- Combining qualitative research with operational data helped distinguish root causes from symptoms.
- Transparency-focused solutions created more value than adding additional self-service capabilities.
- A clear understanding of who pays, what is financed, and what remains outstanding is foundational before introducing more advanced servicing features.

What I Would Do Next

- Run the pilot and validate whether the Installment Agreement Summary reduces the post-creation manual contact rate from 14.6% to 13.1% or lower.
- Analyze remaining support contacts to identify unresolved sources of confusion.
- Expand transparency features with agreement change explanations and lifecycle visibility.
- Only after validating the transparency hypothesis, evaluate additional self-service capabilities such as plan changes, payment deferrals, or status tracking.
